Earnings-Volatility Swing Trading

Holding a position through or around a known volatility catalyst — like an earnings report — for a multi-day directional swing.

What It Is & Why It's Volatile

Swing trading holds positions for several days to a few weeks, aiming to capture a meaningful chunk of a directional move rather than the smaller, faster moves scalpers and day traders target. Earnings-volatility swing trading specifically times entries and exits around known or expected volatility catalysts — quarterly earnings reports being the classic example — where a stock's price range can expand dramatically in a single session as new information gets priced in all at once.

The volatility here is event-driven and often sharply higher than a stock's normal day-to-day range: a single earnings reaction can move a stock as much as several "normal" weeks combined, in either direction, sometimes gapping past any stop-loss set the day before.

Typical Mechanics

  • Entry approaches: some swing traders enter before the catalyst, positioning for an expected reaction (higher risk — the outcome is genuinely unknown); others wait to enter after the reaction, trading the follow-through momentum once direction is confirmed (lower risk, potentially smaller reward).
  • Exit signals: a multi-day price target, a trailing stop once the post-catalyst trend is established, or a fixed holding period (e.g., exit within N sessions regardless of outcome).
  • Holding period: typically 2–10 trading sessions — long enough to let the post-catalyst move play out, short enough to avoid new, unrelated risk building up.
  • Position sizing: should account for gap risk — since a catalyst can move price well beyond a normal day's range overnight, position sizes are usually smaller than for a similarly volatile non-event trade.
  • Gap risk: unlike scalping or day trading, a swing position held through a catalyst can open the next session far away from the prior close, blowing straight through any stop order placed the day before.

Walkthrough Example — SWNG

Swingfield Biotech (SWNG) is the academy's dedicated earnings-volatility sim (~45% annualized volatility with periodic large jump events simulating earnings-style surprises), designed for exactly this drill.

On the Trade page, watch SWNG's 1D and 1W charts. Between jump events, it behaves like a normal mid-volatility stock. When a jump event fires, you'll see a sudden, outsized move — the simulated equivalent of an earnings reaction. A swing trader who entered before the jump captured (or was hurt by) the full gap; one who waits for confirmation after the jump trades the follow-through instead, with less uncertainty but potentially less reward.

Key Risks & Common Mistakes

Stop-losses do not protect you from gap risk. A stop order placed the day before an earnings report can be blown through entirely if the stock gaps past it overnight — your actual fill price could be far worse than your intended stop.
  • Oversizing an earnings-hold position: treating a pre-catalyst position like a normal trade instead of accounting for outsized gap risk.
  • No plan for either outcome: entering before a catalyst without a clear idea of what you'll do if it goes against you.
  • Assuming the initial reaction is the whole move: earnings reactions sometimes reverse in the following sessions as the market digests details beyond the initial headline number.
  • Overholding after the catalyst has passed: staying in the trade well past the volatility event with no fresh justification for holding.

Practice This Strategy

Run this drill on the trading desk with SWNG preselected:

  1. 1. Open SWNG's 1D chart and observe its baseline behavior for a stretch of calm ticks.
  2. 2. Place a modest Market Buy, sized smaller than you might for a non-event trade, to account for jump/gap risk.
  3. 3. Continue watching the chart across multiple ticks — if a sudden jump event occurs, note the size of the move relative to SWNG's normal range.
  4. 4. If the jump was favorable, consider selling into strength rather than assuming the move continues indefinitely.
  5. 5. If no jump occurs after a reasonable number of ticks, practice closing the position anyway — reinforcing a fixed holding-period discipline rather than waiting indefinitely for a catalyst.
